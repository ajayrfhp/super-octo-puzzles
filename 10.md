# Monty Hall Problem: Rigorous Proof & Intuition

## 1. Formal Proof via Bayes' Theorem

### Definitions
Let $C_i$ be the event that the Car is behind Door $i$ (where $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$).
Let $M_j$ be the event that Monty opens Door $j$ (where $j \in \{1, 2, 3\}$).

**Scenario Setup:**
* You choose **Door 1**.
* Monty opens **Door 3** ($M_3$) to reveal a goat.

### Priors (Before Monty acts)
We assume the car is placed randomly:
$$P(C_1) = P(C_2) = P(C_3) = \frac{1}{3}$$

### Likelihoods: $P(M_3 | C_i)$
We analyze the probability of Monty opening Door 3 given the location of the car. Monty is constrained by two rules: he cannot open your door (Door 1), and he cannot open the car door.

1.  **If Car is at Door 1 ($C_1$):**
    Monty can open either Door 2 or Door 3 (both have goats). Assuming he picks randomly:
    $$P(M_3 | C_1) = \frac{1}{2}$$

2.  **If Car is at Door 2 ($C_2$):**
    Monty cannot open Door 1 (your pick). He cannot open Door 2 (car). He **must** open Door 3.
    $$P(M_3 | C_2) = 1$$

3.  **If Car is at Door 3 ($C_3$):**
    Monty cannot open Door 3 because the car is there.
    $$P(M_3 | C_3) = 0$$

### The Posterior: $P(C_i | M_3)$
Using Bayes' Theorem: $P(C_i | M_3) = \frac{P(M_3 | C_i)P(C_i)}{P(M_3)}$

Since the denominators $P(M_3)$ are constant for all $i$, we can compare the numerators:

* **Hypothesis 1 (Stay):**
    $$P(M_3 | C_1)P(C_1) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$$

* **Hypothesis 2 (Switch):**
    $$P(M_3 | C_2)P(C_2) = 1 \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$$

**Normalization:**
Total Probability = $1/6 + 1/3 = 3/6 = 1/2$.

**Final Probabilities:**
* **P(Stay):** $\frac{1/6}{1/2} = \frac{1}{3}$
* **P(Switch):** $\frac{1/3}{1/2} = \frac{2}{3}$

**Conclusion:** Switching doubles your probability of winning.

## 2. Intuitive Explanations

### Intuition A: The "You vs. The World" Split
Ignore the individual doors. Group the game into two sets:
* **Set A (You):** Door 1. (Prob: 1/3)
* **Set B (The World):** Doors 2 & 3. (Prob: 2/3)

Monty's job is to "filter" Set B. He effectively says: *"I am going to check Set B for you. If there is a goat in Set B, I will show it to you. If the car is in Set B (which is 2/3 likely), I will leave the car door closed."*

Monty **cannot** check Set A. His action provides **zero** information about Door 1.
Because he only interacts with Set B, the new information collapses the entire 2/3 probability of "The World" onto the only surviving door in that set: **Door 2**.

### Intuition B: The "Million Doors" Extreme
Imagine there are **1,000,000 doors**.
1.  You pick **Door 1**. Your chance is **1/1,000,000**.
2.  The chance the car is "somewhere else" is **999,999/1,000,000**.

Monty (who knows the truth) begins opening doors. He opens Door 2 (goat), Door 3 (goat)... all the way to Door 777,776.
He intentionally skips **Door 777,777** and leaves it closed.

**The Result:**
* **Door 1:** Still a random 1-in-a-million shot.
* **Door 777,777:** This is the **Chosen Survivor**. It represents the entire 99.9999% probability of the "Other" group. Monty was *forced* to avoid it.

Switching is betting on the 99.9999% chance that you picked wrong initially.
