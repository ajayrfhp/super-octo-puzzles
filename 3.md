# Puzzle Notes: Backpropagation in a Single Sigmoid Neuron

This document outlines the step-by-step derivation of the gradient for a simple neural network, which forms the basis of backpropagation.

---

## 1. The Problem Setup

We have a single-neuron network with one input $x$, one weight $w$, and one true label $y$.

### The Forward Pass

The calculation proceeds in three steps:

1.  **Linear Step:** The input is multiplied by the weight.
    $$z = w \cdot x$$

2.  **Activation:** The result is passed through a sigmoid activation function ($\sigma$).
    $$a = \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$$

3.  **Loss:** The output $a$ is compared to the true label $y$ using a squared error loss function $L$.
    $$L = \frac{1}{2}(a - y)^2$$

### The Goal

Our objective is to find the gradient of the Loss with respect to the weight, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}$. This tells us how a small change in $w$ will affect the final Loss, allowing us to "learn" by updating the weight.

---

## 2. The Solution: The Chain Rule

We cannot compute $\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}$ directly. We must use the **chain rule** to propagate the gradient backward from the loss.

The chain rule breaks the problem down into a product of simpler derivatives:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial z} \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial w}$$

We can solve each of these three parts individually.

---

## 3. Calculating Each Partial Derivative

### Part 1: Loss w.r.t. Activation ($\frac{\partial L}{\partial a}$)

This tells us how the Loss changes as the activation $a$ changes.

* **Equation:** $L = \frac{1}{2}(a - y)^2$
* **Derivative:** We use the power rule.
    $$\frac{\partial L}{\partial a} = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}(a - y)^1 \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial a}(a-y)$$
    $$\frac{\partial L}{\partial a} = (a - y)$$

### Part 2: Activation w.r.t. Linear Step ($\frac{\partial a}{\partial z}$)

This is the famous derivative of the sigmoid function.

* **Equation:** $a = \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$
* **Derivative:** The derivative of the sigmoid function $\sigma(z)$ can be elegantly simplified to $\sigma(z) \cdot (1 - \sigma(z))$.
* In our notation, where $a = \sigma(z)$, this is:
    $$\frac{\partial a}{\partial z} = a \cdot (1 - a)$$

### Part 3: Linear Step w.r.t. Weight ($\frac{\partial z}{\partial w}$)

This tells us how the linear step $z$ changes as the weight $w$ changes.

* **Equation:** $z = w \cdot x$
* **Derivative:** We treat $x$ as a constant for this partial derivative.
    $$\frac{\partial z}{\partial w} = x$$

---

## 4. The Final Gradient

To get our final answer, we multiply the results from all three parts.

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a} \cdot \frac{\partial a}{\partial z} \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial w}$$

Substituting our solutions:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = (a - y) \cdot a(1 - a) \cdot x$$

This final expression is the complete gradient. During training, it would be multiplied by a learning rate and subtracted from the current weight $w$ to minimize the loss.
